About WALTER ↩ Back

WALTER is a browser-based Heart Rate Variability (HRV) analysis tool for undergraduate psychology students. It reads CSV files produced by the HARVEY recorder (including Harvey Duo pairs) and computes time-domain, frequency-domain and Poincaré HRV metrics directly in the browser — no data is ever sent to a server.

The story of WALTER

WALTER grew out of a practical problem. Following earlier research projects that examined HRV using Polar H10 chest sensors, it became clear that recording HRV reliably depended on Polar Sensor Logger — an application available only on Android. Given how common Apple devices are among students and researchers, this was a real barrier. Caspar set out to find an alternative method of collecting data from Polar H10 sensors that was phone-agnostic and kept data-protection concerns at its centre.

Inspired by several IoT projects and after getting to know the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, Caspar developed HARVEY — a dedicated data-acquisition unit. The name is a play on the abbreviation HRV (HRV → Harvey), and also honours William Harvey, the 17th-century English physician who described the circulation of the blood and demonstrated that the heart pumps it. HARVEY uses the Bluetooth adapter on the Pi Zero 2 W to connect to a Polar H10 and writes the collected data straight to CSV. As the unit matured, its use case expanded: from a single H10 to a dual-sensor setup, using an HTTP hotspot to record from two devices simultaneously — and, in principle, from more than two HARVEY units, though this is not yet implemented. This paired configuration became Harvey Duo, which includes a Bluetooth search function to discover local H10 sensors.

After months of development, the HARVEY units were working — and it became apparent that a data-processing tool was needed to turn the raw CSV output into something friendly for students and analysis. Hence WALTER, named after Walter Bradford Cannon, the physiologist known for his work on homeostasis and the "fight-or-flight" response. Caspar wrote a design brief and asked two Computer Science placement students to build it. Together, Caspar and the students — Euan Bell and Kyoka Nomoto — built the initial tool and helped test the Harvey Duo joint-unit system.

What WALTER does, and how

WALTER accepts single files, multiple files, or whole folders. RR intervals are first filtered to a physiologically plausible range (300–2000 ms). Artefactual beats — those deviating more than a set percentage (default 20%) from the preceding beat — are then either interpolated (replaced with the average of their neighbours) or deleted. Where a beat is deleted, WALTER deliberately skips the successive difference across that gap, since the beats on either side were never truly consecutive. Each session is then given a data-quality rating from the percentage of beats retained: Good (≥95%), Fair (85–95%) or Poor (<85%).

What the measures mean

Time-domain metrics (from the cleaned RR series):

  • Mean RR — the average interval between heartbeats, in milliseconds.
  • Mean HR — the average heart rate in beats per minute.
  • RMSSD — the root mean square of successive RR differences; short-term, beat-to-beat variability and the primary index of parasympathetic (vagal) activity.
  • SDNN — the standard deviation of all RR intervals; a global measure of overall variability across the recording.
  • pNN50 — the percentage of successive RR differences greater than 50 ms; another vagally-mediated marker that tends to track with RMSSD.

Frequency-domain metrics (RR series resampled to a 4 Hz grid, then FFT; Task Force 1996 bands):

  • LF — low-frequency power, 0.04–0.15 Hz.
  • HF — high-frequency power, 0.15–0.40 Hz, primarily reflecting respiratory/parasympathetic activity.
  • LF/HF — the ratio of the two, often read as a rough index of autonomic balance. A minimum of 60 seconds of recording is required for a reliable estimate.

Poincaré analysis (each RR interval plotted against the next, with a fitted ellipse):

  • SD1 — dispersion perpendicular to the identity line; short-term variability (closely related to RMSSD).
  • SD2 — dispersion along the identity line; long-term variability.
  • SD1/SD2 — the ratio of the two axes, describing the shape of the scatter.

Duo synchrony:

  • For paired Harvey Duo recordings, WALTER resamples each participant's RMSSD onto a common 1-second grid and computes the Pearson correlation (r) between the two, per phase — a measure of how closely two people's variability moves together. If a SYNC_LOST event is present, the correlation is withheld rather than reported.

Results are presented per-phase and per-group (mean/SD), with charts (tachogram, HR, rolling RMSSD, Poincaré, histogram, accelerometer). WALTER exports an SPSS-ready CSV, a data-quality CSV, and can generate an APA-7 methods/results draft.

ℹ All data, including demographics, is processed entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent to any server.
